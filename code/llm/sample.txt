At the edge of a quiet town stood a narrow workshop with a blue door.
Every morning, Mira unlocked the door, opened the shutters, and placed a
small brass lantern on the workbench. The lantern had no flame. Instead, it
held a wheel, a mirror, and a row of tiny glass tiles.

Mira turned the wheel one step. The first tile glowed amber. She turned it
again, and the next tile glowed too. Soon a pattern travelled along the row:
amber, green, amber, blue. Mira wrote the pattern in her notebook, changed
the position of the mirror, and began again.

A curious robin watched from the windowsill. When the lantern shone blue,
the robin chirped. When it shone green, the robin tilted its head. Mira
noticed the sequence and made a prediction in her notebook before each turn
of the wheel. Some predictions were right and some were wrong. Each mistake
helped her adjust the mirror by a very small amount.

By evening, the lantern could continue several simple patterns. It did not
understand the colours, the workshop, or the robin. It had only learned that
some tiles often followed other tiles. Mira closed the shutters and left one
final note: a useful prediction can come from many small adjustments.
